Persona: Author of competing tool

## Critique

The first paper is useful, but the next paper must not be another "exposure X and hypertension" logistic regression. A competing research workflow would stress-test breadth: different domain joins, different endpoint family, different measurement scale, and different prior literature.

The web search suggests smoking/lipids as a better second paper axis. It is methodologically different because it can use biomarker-verified or questionnaire smoking exposure and continuous lipid outcomes. Prior NHANES work has examined smoking and lipid/lipoprotein concentrations, and recent NHANES-adjacent literature continues to use cotinine/smoking exposure for cardiometabolic outcomes. This gives enough precedent to build a cautious dogfood paper while still forcing the pipeline to handle continuous outcomes and fasting/subsample caveats.

## Actionable Implications

- Paper 2 should use smoking plus lipids if cached variables support it.
- The paper should use a continuous endpoint, not another binary hypertension endpoint.
- QA should require endpoint-type clarity and subsample/fasting limitations if LDL or triglycerides require fasting weights.
- If the local smoking/lipid variables are insufficient, the pipeline should reject the paper idea and record why.

## Evidence That Would Change This Critique

- A second paper with a genuinely different estimand and domain shape, such as kidney albuminuria or socioeconomic gradients in HbA1c.
- A data-access scan showing smoking/lipid variables are too sparse for a strong paper, with a documented replacement idea.
